Portfolio Refresh

The current static site is the antithesis. Moving away from sectioned-CV-as-portfolio toward a single, focused, chat-driven surface where depth is found by interacting, not scrolling. The medium is the message.

Status: spec Owner: Dave Branch: tbd North star: dcurt.is
Hypothesis
A contractor's site that demonstrates the 2026 paradigm shift is itself the portfolio piece.

The why

Visitors to a portfolio spend two minutes, not twenty. The current site is built for the second case — comprehensive, sectioned, scannable, slightly less bland with each block. It works. It's also forgettable.

The 2026 paradigm shift in UX is from listing content to surfacing it through conversation. A chat-driven portfolio puts that shift on display while delivering the actual content, which means the site demonstrates the very craft it's selling. Calm-confident, not calm-pastoral.

North star

Reference site

dcurt.is — calm, opinionated, anti-template. A single bold idea per page, executed with rare restraint. Calm isn't passive; it's deliberate.

Paradigm

Dynamic, chat-driven, results-led UX. Visitors prompt, click, follow up. The interface doesn't lay everything out — it surfaces what was asked for, when it was asked for.

What it keeps · what it deliberately drops

Keeps

  • The hue family — purple, mint, melon, ice. Used boldly, not pastorally.
  • Paytone One headings, Inter body. Voice intact.
  • Hand-drawn touches, used sparingly and with intent.
  • Calm. No popups, no urgency theatre, no AI-typing chrome.
  • Loads instantly. Senior-contractor credibility throughout.

Drops

  • Sectioned page structure. Hero / About / Approach / Skills / Work / Contact — gone.
  • About-me prose. Show by being shown, not by narrating.
  • Named clients and employers in copy.
  • "Listing every skill" energy. Comprehensive over confident.
  • The pastel-pastoral feel. We're going calm-confident, not calm-passive.

Open questions

Surfaced visibly. The answers will land as the work proceeds.

  1. Entry affordance. Blank chat box, or seeded with 3–4 suggested prompts as visible buttons? Strong instinct: visible suggested prompts plus the input box. Explicit invitation, not blank-page anxiety.
  2. The suggested prompts. What are they? Drafting: What kind of work do you do? · Are you available? · Show me something you've built. · What do you care about?
  3. Answer composition. Streamed text? Cards? Mixed media — text plus a project still plus a small fact-card? Has to feel rich, not like a wall of returned text.
  4. Follow-ups. After an answer, do inline next-prompts appear? Is the input always sticky? How does a "conversation" thread visually?
  5. The noscript fallback. Effectively the calm sectioned page we'd otherwise have built. May be where the pastel-pastoral feel is allowed to live.
  6. Real-LLM hatch (v2). If it lands later, where does it sit? An "ask anything" affordance below the curated set, cost-gated by an edge function?
