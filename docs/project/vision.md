# Vision — The Personal Canvas

> *Compass, not contract. Reread when the project drifts and you need to remember why you started. Edit loosely; never rewrite from scratch.*

## The thread

This repo is where my ideas land and become viewable.

A chat surfaces a thought worth keeping. The wiki captures the durable shape of it. Some of those shapes are publishable — first to me, eventually maybe to others. The portfolio is one face of that publishing layer. Other faces will follow when they want to.

## What it's for

- **A clean way to fire up a chat and have an idea become viewable** across phone, iPad, desktop.
- **A place to nurture a brand and style** that's quietly mine — calm, considered, not performatively loud.
- **A thinking surface that survives the chat** — searchable, browseable, returnable-to weeks later without losing the thread.

## Audience

- **Me, first.** The bar is "would I find this interesting six months from now?", not "will this rank on Google?".
- **Future me, second.** A 2027 version of myself opening this on a flight with no context.
- **Selectively, others.** Specific people, sometimes, when an idea is worth showing. Public broadcast is not the goal.

## What it's not

- Not a blog. Writing as performance is not the energy.
- Not a CMS. The chat is the input; the wiki is the durable layer; the published surface is the output.
- Not a SaaS landing page or a personal-brand machine.
- Not a one-time site. Things accrete here over years.

## Posture

Quiet, deliberate, considered. The canonical surface is allowed to be calm without being boring — and the seasonal April Fools build exists as the deliberate foil that proves the choice is intentional.

## How it grows

The pattern is: **chat → wiki → spec → ship → log → repeat.**

The wiki absorbs material. Some of it stays in the wiki forever. Some graduates to a feature spec. Some of those features ship as new faces of the project. Each face that ships gets a stakeholder-friendly HTML overview alongside its working spec, so the project can be read three ways: as raw notes, as a canonical artefact, and as the live thing itself.
